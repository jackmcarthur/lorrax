# 7.5 Assembling Σᶜ(ω)

With $G$'s poles at the band energies and $W$'s at $\pm\Omega_{\mu\nu}$, the
correlation self-energy is the pole convolution of §7.1, organized by which side of
the Fermi level the intermediate state sits on:

$$
\Sigma^c_{\mu\nu}(\omega) \;\sim\; \sum_{n\in occ}
\frac{(\psi\psi^*)_n\, B_{\mu\nu}}{\omega - \varepsilon_n + \Omega_{\mu\nu} - i\eta}
\;+\; \sum_{n\in emp}
\frac{(\psi\psi^*)_n\, B_{\mu\nu}}{\omega - \varepsilon_n - \Omega_{\mu\nu} + i\eta}.
$$

Every denominator has the shape $\omega - S$ with a *composite* energy
$S = \varepsilon_n \pm \Omega$ — a band energy shifted by a pole energy. The
two-sums rule holds because the time-domain representation of $1/(\omega - S)$
factorizes $e^{-S\tau} = e^{-\varepsilon_n\tau} e^{\mp\Omega\tau}$: at each node the
band sum over $n$ and the pole sum over $(\mu\nu)$ proceed independently, contracted
through the same lattice-FFT machinery as χ. The per-node kernel is literally
$G(\text{i}\tau_l) \times W(\tau_l)$ with
$W_{\mu\nu}(\tau) = B_{\mu\nu} e^{-i\Omega_{\mu\nu}\tau}$, projected onto the
requested ω-grid by $e^{\pm i\omega\tau_l}$ factors — a handful of dense
$n_\mu \times n_\mu$ products per node per k-point.

**Branches and who crosses.** The evaluation ω-grid
(`sigma_omega_min_ev`…`max`…`step`, measured from the Fermi reference) is split into
its positive and negative halves, and each half pairs with the occupied and empty
sums — four branches. In each half **exactly one branch can cross**: for $\omega > 0$
the empty-state denominators $\omega - \varepsilon_c - \Omega$ pass through zero
while the occupied ones are sign-definite; for $\omega < 0$ the roles swap. The
sign-definite branch of each half is integrated with a single noncrossing GL rule.

**The crossing branch gets three windows.** The composite energies
$S = \varepsilon + \Omega$ are sorted against a threshold $T = \omega_{max} +
1.5\,\xi$, where $\xi$ is the broadening (`sigma_regularization_ev`, default
0.25 eV — the physical linewidth floor of §4.4):

| Window | Region | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **core** | $\varepsilon \le T$ and $\Omega \le T$: poles can coincide with grid frequencies | crossing HGL sine, bandwidth $A = 2T/\xi$ |
| **a-stripe** | $\varepsilon > T$, $\Omega \le T$: pushed out by the band energy | noncrossing GL on the shifted range |
| **b-slab** | $\Omega > T$ (any $\varepsilon$): pushed out by the pole energy | noncrossing GL on the shifted range |

Only the core — a bounded neighborhood of the physical quasiparticle window — pays
the expensive crossing quadrature; the stripe and slab, which contain the vast
majority of $(n, \mu\nu)$ combinations in any real system, ride cheap exponential
rules. This is the §4.4 "bandwidth-limit first" idea in its production form.
Elements with invalid poles are removed from all three windows and handled by the
static-limit term of §7.4.

**Accumulation and output.** Band projection
$\Sigma_{ij\mathbf{k}}(\omega) = \langle\psi_i|\Sigma(\omega)|\psi_j\rangle$
contracts the spatial kernel with wavefunction values at the centroids; real and
imaginary parts are projected separately (the projection does not commute with
taking real parts — a subtle but load-bearing ordering). Two accumulation
strategies, `sigma_omega_accumulation = kij | kij_stream`, trade a factor of memory
against recomputation and are numerically equivalent to ~1e-13; `auto` picks by
available memory. Results: the QP-equation inputs of §4.5 and, if requested, the
full grid in `sigma_omega_h5_file`.

The bare-exchange part $\Sigma_x$ never enters any of this — it is
frequency-independent, assembled directly from $G_o$ and $V_q$ (§5.3), and added at
the end. The $\mathbf{q}\to 0$ head contribution to Σ is injected analytically from
the fitted head pole (Appendix B).
