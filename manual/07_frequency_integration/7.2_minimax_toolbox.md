# 7.2 The minimax toolbox

All frequency/time integration in LORRAX reduces to three approximation problems,
each solved by a small optimized quadrature. This section is the user-facing account;
Kim, Martyna & Ismail-Beigi, PRB **101**, 035139 (2020) is the foundational reference
and Appendix C documents the shipped tables.

**Problem 1 — noncrossing (GL: Gauss-exponential).** Represent $1/x$ for
$x \in [x_{min}, x_{max}]$, $x$ strictly positive:

$$
\frac{1}{x} \approx \sum_{l=1}^{N} w_l\, e^{-t_l x},
\qquad \varepsilon_N = \max_{x} \Big|\tfrac{1}{x} - \sum_l w_l e^{-t_l x}\Big|.
$$

Nodes and weights are optimized to *equioscillate* the error (a minimax/Remez-type
fit with variable-projection inner solves), and the count grows logarithmically in
the range ratio $R = x_{max}/x_{min}$. LORRAX's empirical error law for the shipped
solver:

$$
\varepsilon \approx 0.31\, e^{-N\,(3.55/\ln R + 0.68)}
$$

— e.g. $R = 10^3$: single-digit-μ accuracy near $N \approx 15$, and every added node
buys a fixed factor. This rule integrates the gapped denominators: the χ transitions
and the Laplace cores of Σ.

**Problem 2 — crossing (HGL: sinusoidal).** Represent $1/x$ for
$x_{min} \le |x| \le x_{max}$ where $x$ changes sign:

$$
\frac{1}{x} \approx \sum_{l=1}^{N} w_l\, \sin(x\,\tau_l),
$$

odd by construction (as $1/x$ is), built via a Hermite-Gauss-Laguerre construction
with node counts scaling roughly linearly in $A = x_{max}/x_{min}$ — the floor
$x_{min}$ here is the broadening/regularization of §4.4, so **crossing rules are the
expensive ones** and the windowing below exists mainly for their benefit. Empirical
law: $\varepsilon \approx e^{-0.93 - 14.25\,N/A}$.

**Problem 3 — imaginary-frequency sampling.** Represent
$x/(x^2 + \omega^2)$ by the same exponential-sum technology — this is what evaluates
$\chi(i\omega_p)$ (needed by the plasmon-pole fit, §7.4) from the same time-domain
propagators, without ever forming χ on a real-frequency grid.

**Windows.** Before any rule is fitted, denominators are partitioned by energy scale
(§4.4): each window spans a bounded ratio, gets its own rule, and the total node
count across windows beats one global rule by a wide margin — logarithmically small
GL rules everywhere the spectrum is gapped, and HGL rules no larger than their
window's $A$ forces. The specific window layout used for Σ is §7.5's business.

**Tables and the one knob.** Fitting a minimax rule from scratch costs seconds to
minutes on the host; production runs therefore draw from **shipped tables**
(`src/common/minimax_assets/`, covering $R$ up to $10^5$ at three grids per decade
and error bounds down to $2\times10^{-7}$) under a strict selection rule: smallest
tabulated range ≥ requested, loosest tabulated error ≤ requested
(`minimax_target_error`), node count ≤ `minimax_max_nodes`. Anything not covered
falls back to the exact solver (or set `regenerate_minimax_tables` to force it). The
error conventions — absolute $L_\infty$ on the scaled interval, rescaled to the
physical one — are spelled out in Appendix C.

The practical upshot a user should retain: **`minimax_target_error` is the single
accuracy dial for all of this machinery**, its cost response is logarithmic in the
noncrossing sector and linear in the crossing sector, and the defaults
($10^{-6}$, 64-node cap) are set so that quadrature error is negligible against the
physical approximations (basis rank §5.5, pole model §7.4) in any sane calculation.
