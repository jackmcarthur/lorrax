# 7.1 Convolutions of two pole sets

The two dynamical objects of GW are hard for the same reason. The polarizability is
a frequency convolution of the occupied and empty pole sets of $G$,

$$
\chi^0(\omega) \sim \sum_{v,c} \left[
\frac{1}{\omega - (\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v) + i0^+}
- \frac{1}{\omega + (\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v) - i0^+} \right]
\otimes (\text{pair densities}),
$$

and the correlation self-energy convolves the poles of $G$ with the poles of $W$,

$$
\Sigma_c(\omega) \sim \sum_{n} \sum_{p}
\frac{(\text{residue}_n \otimes \text{residue}_p)}
{\omega - \varepsilon_n \mp \Omega_p \pm i0^+},
$$

$n$ over all bands (occupied and empty with opposite pole prescriptions, the
$\Sigma^-$/$\Sigma^+$ branches), $p$ over the excitations of $W$.

Two consequences organize the chapter. First, time-domain separability is used
twice: each denominator factorizes under the representations of §4.2/§4.4, and the
shared nodes let the band sum over $n$ and the pole sum over $p$ proceed
independently (the two-sums rule applied to a convolution). Second, $W$ must itself
be a sum of poles $\{\Omega_p, R_p\}$; the exact $W$ is one (a pole per
charge-neutral excitation, the same excitations the BSE resolves, §9.2), and a
plasmon-pole model compresses that sum (§7.4).

Order of the chapter: quadrature toolbox (§7.2), χ (§7.3), pole compression of $W$
(§7.4), assembly of $\Sigma_c(\omega)$ (§7.5), accuracy (§7.6). The $q\to0$ head is
a finite-size correction, Appendix B.
