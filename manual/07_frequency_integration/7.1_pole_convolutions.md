# 7.1 The problem: convolutions of two pole sets

Chapter 4 established the constraint — everything must reduce to single-occupancy
band sums glued by $O(N^0)$ nodes — and §4.4 gave the working picture. This chapter
is the quantitative machinery. It helps to state, once, what makes the two dynamical
objects of GW hard, because it is the *same* thing twice:

**The polarizability** is a frequency convolution of the occupied and empty pole
sets of $G$:

$$
\chi^0(\omega) \sim \sum_{v,c} \left[
\frac{1}{\omega - (\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v) + i0^+}
- \frac{1}{\omega + (\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v) - i0^+} \right]
\otimes (\text{pair densities}),
$$

poles at every interband transition energy, weighted by products of orbitals.

**The correlation self-energy** is a frequency convolution of the poles of $G$ with
the poles of $W$:

$$
\Sigma_c(\omega) \sim \sum_{n} \sum_{p}
\frac{(\text{residue}_n \otimes \text{residue}_p)}
{\omega - \varepsilon_n \mp \Omega_p \pm i0^+},
$$

with $n$ running over *all* bands (occupied and empty enter with opposite pole
prescriptions — the $\Sigma^-$/$\Sigma^+$ branches) and $p$ over the excitations of
the screened interaction.

Two structural conclusions drive everything in this chapter:

1. **Time-domain separability is used twice.** Each denominator above factorizes
   under the $1/x \to \int e^{-x\tau}$ (or sinusoidal) representations of §4.2/§4.4,
   once for χ and once for Σ. The shared quadrature nodes are what let the band sum
   over $n$ and the pole sum over $p$ be performed *independently* — the two-sums
   rule, applied to a convolution.

2. **W must itself be a sum of poles.** For the Σ convolution to have the form
   above, the screened interaction needs an explicit pole representation
   $\{\Omega_p, R_p\}$. This is not an imposition on the physics — the exact $W$ *is*
   a sum of poles, one per charge-neutral excitation of the interacting system (the
   same excitations a Bethe–Salpeter calculation targets, §9.2). A plasmon-pole model
   simply compresses that sum; how LORRAX builds it is §7.4.

The chapter proceeds in pipeline order: the quadrature toolbox (§7.2), χ at the
frequencies the toolbox needs (§7.3), the pole compression of W (§7.4), the assembly
of Σ_c(ω) (§7.5), and what it all costs in accuracy terms (§7.6). The q→0 head of W
— a finite-size correction, not a frequency-integration issue — stays in Appendix B.
