# 7.3 The polarizability and the Dyson solve

The screened-interaction pipeline needs χ at exactly two kinds of frequency: the
static point and one imaginary frequency (the plasmon-pole probe of §7.4). Both come
from the same time-domain propagators; χ is never formed on a real-frequency grid.

**Static χ.** The transitions $x = \varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v$ occupy
$[x_{min}, x_{max}]$ = [minimum gap, widest transition]. A noncrossing GL rule (§7.2,
Problem 1) is fitted on that interval once per run, and

$$
\chi^0(\mathbf{r}_\mu,\mathbf{r}_\nu; 0) \;=\; -\sum_l \alpha_l\;
G_o(\mathbf{r}_\mu,\mathbf{r}_\nu; \tau_l)\, G_e^*(\mathbf{r}_\mu,\mathbf{r}_\nu; \tau_l),
$$

the §4.2 factorization evaluated at the rule's nodes, assembled per lattice vector
$\mathbf{R}$ and FFT'd to all $\mathbf{q}$ at once (§4.3).

**χ at an imaginary frequency.** For $\chi^0(i\omega_p)$ the target function changes
but nothing else does: the resonant + antiresonant pair sums to
$2x/(x^2+\omega_p^2)$, so the toolbox fits $x/(x^2+\omega_p^2) \approx
\sum_l \alpha_l e^{-\tau_l x}$ (§7.2, Problem 3) on the *same* transition interval,
and the identical $G_o G_e^*$ contraction with the new weights delivers
$\chi^0(i\omega_p)$. Imaginary frequencies keep the integrand in the decaying
(noncrossing) regime — this is why the plasmon-pole probe is placed on the imaginary
axis rather than the real one.

**The Dyson solve.** With $\chi^0$ and the Coulomb matrix $V_q$ (§5.3) in the ISDF
basis, the screened interaction at each needed frequency solves

$$
\big(I - V_q\, \kappa\,\chi^0_q\big)\, W_q \;=\; V_q,
\qquad
\kappa = \frac{2}{\sqrt{N_k}\; n_{\text{spin}}\, n_{\text{spinor}}},
$$

an $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ dense linear system per $\mathbf{q}$ (LU-based in the
default backend), after which the correlation part $W^c = W - v$ is what the
self-energy machinery consumes. The prefactor κ carries the lattice-FT and spin
normalization conventions; it is quoted here because it is the single most common
source of factor-of-two bugs when comparing against other codes' χ conventions.

Two `screening_solver` backends implement the same mathematics:

- **`jax_native`** (default): the direct solve above, sharded over the device mesh.
- **`cublasmp_ffi`** (low-memory): a symmetrized form — Cholesky-factor
  $v = XX^\dagger$, solve $H = I - X^\dagger(\kappa\chi)X$, reconstruct
  $W = X H^{-1} X^\dagger$ — fused into the distributed cuBLASMp path (§2.3), which
  avoids materializing intermediate $n_\mu^2$ products at full precision replicas.

The $\mathbf{q}\to 0$ element of $W$ — where $V_q$ diverges and χ vanishes — is *not*
solved this way; its head is fitted separately from dipole data (§6.4, Appendix B)
and injected analytically into Σ.
