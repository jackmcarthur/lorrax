# 7.4 Compressing W into poles: the Godby–Needs construction

Section 7.1 explained why Σ needs $W$ as an explicit sum of poles, and why that is
physically natural: the exact $W^c(\omega)$ *is* a sum of poles, one per
charge-neutral excitation of the interacting system (the excitations a BSE
calculation resolves individually, §9.2). A plasmon-pole model compresses this to
**one effective pole per ISDF matrix element**:

$$
W^c_{\mu\nu}(\omega) \;=\; \frac{2B_{\mu\nu}\,\Omega_{\mu\nu}}{\omega^2 - \Omega_{\mu\nu}^2}
\;=\; B_{\mu\nu}\left[\frac{1}{\omega-\Omega_{\mu\nu}} - \frac{1}{\omega+\Omega_{\mu\nu}}\right],
$$

a pole pair at $\pm\Omega_{\mu\nu}$ with residue $B_{\mu\nu}$. (Conventions differ
across the literature and across older LORRAX documents; the residue form above is
what the code implements, with $W^c(0) = -2B/\Omega$.)

**The Godby–Needs fit.** Two samples determine the two parameters per element — the
static value and one *imaginary*-frequency probe $z = i\omega_p$
(`ppm_omega_p`, default 2 Ry):

$$
\Omega^2_{\mu\nu} = \frac{-z^2\, W^c_{\mu\nu}(z)}{W^c_{\mu\nu}(0) - W^c_{\mu\nu}(z)},
\qquad
B_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\, W^c_{\mu\nu}(0)\,\Omega_{\mu\nu},
$$

with $\Omega = \sqrt{\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega^2}$. It can be shown that sampling on the
imaginary axis, where $W^c$ is smooth and sign-definite, makes this two-point fit far
better conditioned than any real-axis sampling — that is the Godby–Needs insight
[Godby & Needs, PRL **62**, 1169 (1989)], and results are famously insensitive to the
probe placement for $\omega_p$ on the plasmonic scale.

**Validity and the invalid modes.** A pole pair is *valid* when
$\mathrm{Re}\,\Omega^2 > 0$ (and the fit denominators are finite). Matrix elements
that fail — typically small, rapidly oscillating off-diagonal entries — are counted
in the log's fit census and handled per `ppm_invalid_mode`:

- **`static_limit`** (default; BGW `invalid_gpp_mode = 3`): the dynamical pole is
  dropped and replaced by its exact $\Omega \to \infty$ limit — an ω-independent
  static-COHSEX contribution, $-\langle G_o W^c(0)\rangle + \tfrac12\langle G_{RI}
  W^c(0)\rangle$ restricted to the invalid elements. The physically sensible default:
  invalid elements are treated as instantaneously screened.
- **`zero`** (BGW mode 0): drop the elements entirely.
- **`2ry`** (BGW mode 2): keep them as live poles at `ppm_fallback_omega`.

**Alternatives, briefly.** The Hybertsen–Louie GPP fixes its pole from the static
dielectric matrix plus the f-sum rule instead of a second frequency sample; LORRAX
retains it (`ppm_model = "hl"`, with a real probe placed above all transitions) for
comparison studies, and Appendix G's references derive both models' moment
conditions. The natural systematic *extension* — promoting the single pole to a
small multipole set fitted at several imaginary frequencies, recovering
full-frequency accuracy for metals and satellite physics — reuses this machinery
with a pole index and is on the roadmap.
