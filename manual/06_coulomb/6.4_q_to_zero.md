# 6.4 The q → 0 limit, in one paragraph

At $\mathbf{q}\to 0$ two zeros collide: the bare kernel diverges as $1/q^2$
(§6.2 handled its cell average), while the head of the polarizability *vanishes* as
$q^2$ — a consequence of gauge invariance (equivalently, the f-sum rule), with the
coefficient set by the **transition dipoles** of the occupied–empty manifold. Their product, the head of the
screened interaction, is therefore *finite but not computable from the discrete grid
alone*: the code must know the $q^2$ coefficient, which it builds from dipole matrix
elements (computed at the DFT level by `psp.get_dipole_mtxels`, §9.1) via the
s-tensor machinery, or accepts as explicit overrides (`wcoul0_source`,
`whead_0freq`, `whead_imfreq`) — including the dynamical (imaginary-frequency) head
needed by the GN-PPM fit.

That is all a user needs on first reading: *the $q=0$ head of $W$ is special, LORRAX
computes it from dipoles by default, and the knobs exist mainly for BGW parity work.*
The delicate physics — wings, the anisotropic angular average, dimensionality
differences, the dynamical head fit, and every formula — is **Appendix B**, kept out
of the main line deliberately: it is the one piece of GW bookkeeping whose details
have historically consumed more reader attention than its importance warrants, and
nothing else in Chapters 4–9 depends on those details.
