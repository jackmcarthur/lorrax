# 6.3 Cutoffs: pair densities carry twice the wavevector

A plane-wave DFT calculation with energy cutoff $E_{\text{cut}}$ contains
wavefunction components up to $G_{\max} = \sqrt{E_{\text{cut}}}$ (Ry units). But GW
never integrates bare wavefunctions against the Coulomb kernel — it integrates
**pair densities**, and a product of two wavefunctions is a convolution in
reciprocal space:

$$
\big[\psi^*_m \psi_n\big](\mathbf{G}) \;=\; \sum_{\mathbf{G}'}
c_m^*(\mathbf{G}'-\mathbf{G})\, c_n(\mathbf{G}'),
$$

which carries wavevectors up to $2G_{\max}$ — an energy cutoff of
$(2G_{\max})^2 = 4\,E_{\text{cut}}$. This is the same statement as the familiar DFT
rule that the charge density needs `ecutrho` $= 4\times$ `ecutwfc`: densities are
products, and products have twice the bandwidth.

**LORRAX resolves the full pair bandwidth by default.** The ISDF vectors
$\zeta_{q,\mu}$ are fitted in real space on the FFT grid and transformed *as
densities*, so the Coulomb matrix elements of §5.3 can be — and by default are —
integrated over G-spheres up to $4E_{\text{cut}}$, matching the DFT density cutoff
exactly. The controlling inputs:

- **`bare_coulomb_cutoff`** (Ry) — the G-sphere for Coulomb matrix elements.
  **Default: $4\times$ the wavefunction cutoff** (full pair bandwidth).
  Physically meaningful range: anything from small values up to $4E_{\text{cut}}$.
- **`zeta_cutoff`** (Ry) — optionally restricts the G-sphere on which $\zeta$ itself
  is represented/stored; a compression knob for the disk-resident ζ store, normally
  left unset (full grid).

> **BGW correspondence — read before any comparison.** BerkeleyGW's G-space pipeline
> builds $\chi$ and $\Sigma$ from wavefunction coefficients directly, so its
> `epsilon_cutoff`/`screened_coulomb_cutoff` is *capped at the wavefunction cutoff*
> $E_{\text{cut}}$ — the region $E_{\text{cut}} < |{\bf q+G}|^2 \le 4E_{\text{cut}}$
> that LORRAX includes by default is simply inaccessible there. Consequently the two
> codes' *defaults do not match*: LORRAX at $4E_{\text{cut}}$, BGW at
> $E_{\text{cut}}$-scale settings. **For any parity study, set
> `bare_coulomb_cutoff` explicitly to the BGW value.** Forgetting this is the single
> most common source of "mysterious" few-tens-of-meV discrepancies in exchange
> energies.

Practical guidance: for production physics, leave the default (the extra bandwidth is
nearly free — the kernel decays as $1/G^2$ and the FFTs are already paid for); for
convergence studies treat `bare_coulomb_cutoff` as an ordinary variational dial
