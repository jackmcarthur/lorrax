# 6.1 The kernel and its truncation

In BerkeleyGW conventions (Rydberg units),

$$
v_q(\mathbf{G}) \;=\; \frac{8\pi}{|\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}|^2}\;
T_{\text{cell}}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}),
$$

with the truncation factor $T_{\text{cell}}$ set by `sys_dim`:

- `sys_dim = 3` (bulk): $T = 1$.
- `sys_dim = 2` (slab): $T = 1 -
  e^{-|\mathbf{q}_\parallel+\mathbf{G}_\parallel| L_z/2}\cos(G_z L_z/2)$,
  interactions cut at half the vertical cell so periodic images along $z$ do not
  screen each other. Mandatory for 2D materials; without it, gaps and exciton
  binding depend on vacuum thickness. Valid only if the slab and its evanescent
  tails fit within $L_z/2$; under-padding reintroduces image interactions smoothly,
  so converge the vacuum.
- `sys_dim = 0` (box): interaction confined to the Wigner–Seitz box, for isolated
  systems. The kernel (a port of BerkeleyGW's cell-box truncation) is implemented
  but not yet wired through the production $V_q$ driver (§1.2); the driver accepts
  2 and 3.

Truncation changes the long-wavelength physics qualitatively (in 2D,
$\epsilon(q) \to 1$ as $q \to 0$ instead of a bulk constant) and everything
downstream inherits it; set `sys_dim` to the physical dimensionality. It costs
nothing: a multiplicative factor per $(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})$, applied identically
in exchange, screening, and BSE.

**Implementation.** The kernels live in the `vcoul` service —
`services/vcoul/src/vcoul/{bulk_3d,slab_2d,box_0d}.py`, one `CoulombKernel`
per dimensionality — and the single driver that evaluates them on a per-q
G-sphere is `vcoul.v_qG_table`. The production V_q path reaches that driver
through `gw.compute_vcoul.compute_v_q_per_G`, which is a thin dispatcher and
holds no arithmetic of its own; the dimension-independent capabilities
(`vcoul_cutoff_ry` masking, the `argmin |q+G|` head-slot injection, the
`(n_q, ngkmax)` float64 contract) are implemented once in the driver rather
than once per caller.

`src/gw/coulomb/` is a **translation layer, not a second implementation**: it
re-exports the service's names and converts the `(wfn, meta)` call shape the
tree writes into the service's `CoulombGeometry` + explicit `kgrid`. Read
`vcoul.base` and `vcoul.minibz` for the physics. *(This paragraph used to
name `src/gw/coulomb/{bulk_3d,slab_2d,box_0d}.py` as "Implementation", from
the era when those files held the arithmetic AND the live path ran a separate
`compute_v_q_per_G` — two kernels with disjoint capabilities and only one
caller between them. The extraction closed that; the page had not caught up.)*

> **BGW correspondence.** `sys_dim` 3/2/0 = no truncation /
> `cell_slab_truncation` / `cell_box_truncation`. Wire (1D) truncation is not
> implemented.

The $q\to0$ region needs two special treatments: the cell average of the diverging
kernel (§6.2) and the head of the screened interaction (§6.4).
