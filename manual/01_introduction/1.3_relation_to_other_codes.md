# 1.3 Relation to other codes

**Quantum ESPRESSO** is LORRAX's DFT front end. The mean-field starting point — SCF
density, NSCF wavefunctions on the GW k-grid, and the band manifold — is generated by
`pw.x` and exported with `pw2bgw.x`. LORRAX reads the resulting BerkeleyGW-format
`WFN.h5` directly and inherits QE's plane-wave and pseudopotential conventions
(fully-relativistic ONCV pseudopotentials are the standard choice, in keeping with the
spinor-first design).

**BerkeleyGW** is the reference implementation LORRAX validates against, and the
source of most of its file-format and convention choices: `WFN.h5` layout, symmetry
conventions, Coulomb-divergence handling, and `eqp.dat`-style output. Where the
formalisms can be made to coincide (static COHSEX; plasmon-pole GW at matched
parameters), LORRAX and BerkeleyGW agree to meV-level tolerances, and the automated
test suite pins several of these comparisons permanently. Appendix E gives the recipe
(and the traps) for matched comparisons.

Relative to full-frequency codes built on imaginary-axis sampling plus analytic
continuation (VASP GW, Questaal, GreenX-based stacks) or contour deformation
(Yambo, ABINIT), LORRAX's frequency treatment is closest in spirit to the
imaginary-*time* stochastic and low-scaling formulations, but with deterministic
minimax quadrature and results delivered directly on the real axis (Chapter 7).

## LORRAX vs. legacy BerkeleyGW — when to use which

Since most prospective users arrive from BerkeleyGW, we state the trade plainly.

**Where LORRAX wins:**

- **Runtime scaling in k-points: $O(N_k \log N_k)$.** All Brillouin-zone sums are
  executed as FFT convolutions over the Born–von Kármán lattice (§4.3). Legacy
  G-space codes pay $O(N_k^2)$ in the k-sum of every $\chi^0_\mathbf{q}$ assembly.
  For dense k-grids (2D materials, magnetic superlattices, moiré cells sampled fine),
  this is the dominant advantage.
- **System-size scaling: $O(N^3)$** vs. $O(N^4)$, from the two-sums rule (§4.2) plus
  ISDF compression (Ch. 5).
- **Real-axis dynamics without analytic continuation**, with the full
  $\Sigma_{ij\mathbf{k}}(\omega)$ available on a user-chosen grid, at
  quadrature error controlled by a single input (§7.6).
- **Bispinor GW** — the Breit/current-current channel (Ch. 8) has, to our knowledge,
  no counterpart in production GW codes.
- **Dielectric cutoff beyond the wavefunction cutoff.** The real-space pipeline
  supports screening cutoffs up to the full pair-density bandwidth, 4× the
  wavefunction energy cutoff — matching the DFT charge-density cutoff — where
  BerkeleyGW's G-space pipeline is capped at the wavefunction cutoff (§6.3).
- **GPU-native execution** with one code path from a laptop to a multi-node cluster.

**Where legacy BerkeleyGW wins:**

- **Minimum memory workspace.** LORRAX must hold the sharded ISDF problem (the ζ-fit
  workspaces and the $(\mu,\nu)$ matrices for the whole calculation) across the
  device pool at once; the entry ticket for a given system is generally *more
  processors* than a BGW run of the same system needs to merely start.
- **Memory scaling in k-points is linear.** R-space storage of lattice-resolved
  two-point objects makes device memory grow as $O(N_k)$. BerkeleyGW's
  one-$\mathbf{q}$-at-a-time pipeline does not hold the whole zone in memory. The
  runtime/memory trade in k-points is a deliberate design position, not an oversight:
  LORRAX spends memory (the cheap, poolable resource on modern GPU clusters) to buy
  the $N_k \log N_k$ runtime.
- **Spin flexibility.** BerkeleyGW runs spinless and collinear spin-polarized
  calculations at their natural cost. LORRAX is spinor-only: silicon runs as a
  (trivially) spin-orbit-coupled system.
- **Front-end breadth.** BerkeleyGW accepts many DFT codes; LORRAX currently accepts
  Quantum ESPRESSO via the BGW format.
- **Track record.** Two decades of published validation vs. LORRAX's younger record.

**Rule of thumb:** dense k-grids, GPU allocations, SOC-heavy or magnetic systems, full
Σ(ω) needs → LORRAX. Minimal hardware, spinless/collinear systems, exotic DFT front
ends → legacy BGW.
