# 4.2 The two-sums rule

Why is conventional GW $O(N^4)$? Write the static polarizability the textbook way
(Adler–Wiser; k labels suppressed):

$$
\chi^0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';0) \;=\; -\sum_{v}^{occ}\sum_{c}^{emp}
\frac{\rho_{vc}(\mathbf{r})\,\rho_{vc}^*(\mathbf{r}')}
{\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v} \;+\; (\mathbf{r}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{r}')^*,
\qquad
\rho_{vc}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_s \psi^*_{v,s}(\mathbf{r})\,\psi_{c,s}(\mathbf{r}).
$$

Two bookkeeping notes, once and for all. The spin sum lives *inside* the pair
density $\rho_{vc}$: for two-component spinors there is no overall spin factor of 2
(the familiar textbook 2 is the collinear spin-degeneracy special case, which the
spinor trace of §5.2 replaces). And the transposed term is the antiresonant time
ordering — handled by the identical machinery, so we drop it from the displays from
here on.

The energy denominator couples $v$ to $c$: the sum runs over **pairs**, $N_v N_c \sim
O(N^2)$ of them, each contributing an outer product over spatial points. With $O(N)$
spatial resolution per axis that is $O(N^4)$ — and no rearrangement of a
$1/(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v)$ weight can decouple the indices.

The denominator is the *only* thing coupling them. So LORRAX removes it with an exact
integral representation:

$$
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v} \;=\; \int_0^\infty d\tau\;
e^{-(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v)\tau}
\;\approx\; \sum_{l=1}^{N_\tau} w_l\, e^{-(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v) t_l},
$$

valid because $\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v > 0$ across the gap. At each quadrature node
$t_l$ the exponential **factorizes**, $e^{-(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v)t_l} =
e^{-\varepsilon_c t_l}\, e^{+\varepsilon_v t_l}$, and the pair sum falls apart into a
product of two independent single-occupancy sums:

$$
\chi^0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';0) \;=\; -\sum_l w_l\;
\underbrace{\Big[\sum_v \psi_v(\mathbf{r})\psi_v^*(\mathbf{r}')\,e^{\varepsilon_v t_l}\Big]}_{G_o(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t_l)}\;
\underbrace{\Big[\sum_c \psi_c^*(\mathbf{r})\psi_c(\mathbf{r}')\,e^{-\varepsilon_c t_l}\Big]}_{G_e^*(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t_l)},
$$

i.e. $\chi^0(t_l) = -\, G_o(t_l)\odot G_e^*(t_l)$, a **pointwise product of two band
sums** (each bracket is a $2\times 2$ spin matrix for spinors; the product carries
the implied spin trace of §5.2). Each band sum costs $O(N \cdot N_r^{2})$ — in the
compressed basis, $O(N\, n_\mu^2) \sim O(N^3)$ — and there are only $N_\tau$ of them.

This works as a scaling cure *only if* $N_\tau$ does not grow with system size. It
doesn't: $N_\tau$ depends on the **energy range** of the denominators (the ratio of
band width to band gap), which is a property of the material's energy scales, not of
how many atoms are in the cell. A few tens of nodes cover realistic ranges for these
decaying-exponential rules at $\mu$eV-level quadrature error; the sign-changing
denominators of the self-energy need larger real-axis rules (§4.4, §7.2), but their
size is set by the same material energy scales. We write this as
$N_\tau \sim O(N^0)$.

Hence the design law the entire code obeys, which this manual will point back to
repeatedly:

> **The two-sums rule.** No quantity in LORRAX is ever assembled by summing over
> valence–conduction *pairs*. Every object is built from band sums over a single
> occupancy class — a valence sum, or a conduction sum — combined at an $O(N^0)$
> set of shared time or frequency nodes.

Everything downstream is an instance of it. The polarizability above is the cleanest
case. The correlation self-energy needs the same trick twice, because there the poles
of $G$ meet the poles of $W$ — that is Chapter 7, where the quadrature also has to
survive denominators that *change sign*. The BSE matvec (§9.2) and the bispinor
channels (Ch. 8) inherit the rule wholesale. And whenever an equation in this manual
looks like it is about to sum over $v,c$ jointly, a note in the margin will show where
the factorization actually happens.

The rule also dictates *what must be stored*: single-occupancy propagators
$G_{o/e}(\mathbf{r}_\mu, \mathbf{r}_\nu; t_l)$ — dense $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ matrices,
a handful per k-point. That storage shape is the origin of both LORRAX's distribution
strategy (two-point objects sharded over their two spatial axes, Ch. 13) and its
memory economics (§1.3, §4.3).
