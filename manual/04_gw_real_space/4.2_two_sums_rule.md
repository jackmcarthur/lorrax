# 4.2 The two-sums rule

The Adler–Wiser static polarizability (k labels suppressed),

$$
\chi^0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';0) \;=\; -\sum_{v}^{occ}\sum_{c}^{emp}
\frac{\rho_{vc}(\mathbf{r})\,\rho_{vc}^*(\mathbf{r}')}
{\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v} \;+\; (\mathbf{r}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{r}')^*,
\qquad
\rho_{vc}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_s \psi^*_{v,s}(\mathbf{r})\,\psi_{c,s}(\mathbf{r}),
$$

is a sum over $N_v N_c \sim O(N^2)$ pairs, each an outer product over space:
$O(N^4)$, and no rearrangement of the $1/(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v)$ weight
decouples the indices. (The spin sum lives inside $\rho_{vc}$; for two-component
spinors there is no overall factor of 2, the textbook 2 being the collinear special
case. The transposed term is the antiresonant time ordering, handled identically
and dropped from the displays below.)

The denominator is removed by the exact representation

$$
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v} \;=\; \int_0^\infty d\tau\;
e^{-(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v)\tau}
\;\approx\; \sum_{l=1}^{N_\tau} w_l\, e^{-(\varepsilon_c-\varepsilon_v) t_l},
$$

valid since $\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v > 0$. At each node the exponential
factorizes and the pair sum separates:

$$
\chi^0(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';0) \;=\; -\sum_l w_l\;
G_o(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t_l)\; G_e^*(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t_l),
$$

a pointwise product of two band sums (spin trace implied). Each band sum costs
$O(N n_\mu^2) \sim O(N^3)$; there are $N_\tau$ of them, and $N_\tau$ depends on the
ratio of band width to band gap, a material property independent of cell size, so
$N_\tau \sim O(N^0)$. A few tens of nodes reach $\mu$eV quadrature error for the
decaying rules; the sign-changing denominators of Σ need larger real-axis rules
(§4.4, §7.2) governed by the same energy scales.

> **The two-sums rule.** No quantity in LORRAX is assembled by summing over
> valence–conduction pairs. Every object is built from band sums over a single
> occupancy class, combined at an $O(N^0)$ set of shared time or frequency nodes.

Σ applies the trick twice, since the poles of $G$ meet the poles of $W$ (Ch. 7);
the BSE matvec (§9.2) and the bispinor channels (Ch. 8) inherit the rule unchanged.
The stored objects it implies, dense $G_{o/e}(\mathbf{r}_\mu,\mathbf{r}_\nu;t_l)$
matrices, set the distribution strategy (Ch. 13) and the memory economics (§1.3,
§4.3).
