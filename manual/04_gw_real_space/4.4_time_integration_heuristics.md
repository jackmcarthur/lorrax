# 4.4 Time integration: the working picture

The denominators $x = \varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v$ span three to four decades,
from the gap to the band width, and one shared node set $\{t_l, w_l\}$ must
represent $1/x$ uniformly over that range. Minimax rules do this optimally
(worst-case error minimized over the interval), with node counts growing only
logarithmically in the range ratio $R = x_{max}/x_{min}$; that logarithm is why
$N_\tau \sim O(N^0)$.

Two kinds of denominators, two time axes. Noncrossing denominators keep one sign
(every $\varepsilon_c - \varepsilon_v$ in $\chi^0$ is positive): $e^{-x\tau}$
decays for all terms and the integral lives on the imaginary-time axis. Crossing
denominators change sign (self-energy combinations like
$\omega - \varepsilon_c - \Omega$ pass through zero as $\omega$ sweeps the
quasiparticle range): a decaying representation is impossible, and these terms are
handled on the real-time axis, where $1/x$ is represented by a sum of sinusoids
$\sum_l w_l \sin(x\tau_l)$.

A finite sinusoid sum is bandwidth-limited: it reproduces $1/x$ only on an annulus
$x_{min} \le |x| \le x_{max}$. The upper edge is the band width. The lower edge is
the gap in the noncrossing case; in the crossing case $x$ passes through zero at
resonances and the floor is an imposed broadening (`sigma_regularization_ev`, a
physical linewidth, §7.5). Denominators are therefore sorted into windows of
bounded range before integrating: noncrossing rules stay logarithmically small,
crossing rules grow roughly linearly in the bandwidth-to-broadening ratio (tens to
of order a hundred nodes), and the window decomposition keeps each rule minimal.

Node placement, weights, and window edges derive automatically from the spectrum
and one input, `minimax_target_error` (§7.2, App. C).
