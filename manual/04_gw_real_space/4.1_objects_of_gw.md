# 4.1 The objects of GW, drawn in real space

> **Reader note.** This chapter is written as if every quantity were evaluated
> explicitly on the real-space FFT grid: two-point functions $K(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$
> with $\mathbf{r}$ running over $N_r$ grid points. In the actual code, $\mathbf{r}$ and
> $\mathbf{r}'$ run over a far smaller set of *interpolation points*
> $\{\mathbf{r}_\mu\}$ introduced in Chapter 5 — a few thousand points chosen so that
> products of wavefunctions are faithfully represented. **Nothing in this chapter
> changes under that substitution.** Read $(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ as
> $(\mathbf{r}_\mu,\mathbf{r}_\nu)$ throughout, and postpone caring about it until
> Chapter 5.

GW corrects the DFT band energies by replacing the exchange-correlation potential
$V_{xc}$ with the self-energy $\Sigma = iGW$: an energy-dependent, non-local, non-Hermitian
operator built from the system's own screened Coulomb interaction. Every ingredient is
a **two-point kernel** in space:

$$
G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t), \qquad
\chi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';t), \qquad
W(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\omega), \qquad
\Sigma(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\omega).
$$

The one-shot loop computes them in sequence:

1. **Green's function** from the DFT bands. The only two combinations the formalism
   ever needs are the occupied-only and empty-only propagators,

   $$
   G_o(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\tau) = \sum_{v}^{occ} \psi_v(\mathbf{r})\,\psi_v^*(\mathbf{r}')\,
   e^{\varepsilon_v \tau},
   \qquad
   G_e(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}';\tau) = \sum_{c}^{emp} \psi_c(\mathbf{r})\,\psi_c^*(\mathbf{r}')\,
   e^{-\varepsilon_c \tau},
   $$

   with energies measured from the chemical potential so both decay as $\tau$ grows.
   For two-component spinors each $\psi\psi^*$ is a $2\times2$ matrix in spin space;
   products of two propagators carry an implied spin trace (§5.2). Note each is a sum
   over **one occupancy class only** — this is the load-bearing structural fact of
   the whole code (§4.2).

2. **Irreducible polarizability** $\chi^0 = -iG\,G$: an electron and a hole propagating
   together. In the time domain this is literally a pointwise product of the two
   propagators above.

3. **Screened interaction** from the Dyson equation
   $W = v + v\,\chi^0\,W$, solved as a linear system in the compressed spatial basis
   at the few frequencies the self-energy integration needs (Ch. 7). The bare $v$ and
   its geometry-dependent truncation are Chapter 6.

4. **Self-energy** $\Sigma = iGW$, split as exchange $\Sigma_x = -G_o v$ (bare,
   frequency-independent) plus correlation $\Sigma_c(\omega)$ (dynamic, built from
   $W - v$ so that bare exchange is counted exactly once). In the *static COHSEX*
   approximation $\Sigma_c$ collapses to a screened-exchange-*minus*-bare-exchange
   term plus a Coulomb-hole term at $\omega=0$; in *GN-PPM* mode the full frequency
   dependence is restored through a plasmon-pole representation of $W$ (§7.4–7.5).
   (BerkeleyGW's printed SX/CH decomposition groups these terms differently from
   LORRAX's internal branches — only totals are cross-comparable; Appendix E.)

5. **Band projection and the quasiparticle equation.** Matrix elements
   $\Sigma_{ij\mathbf{k}}(\omega) = \langle \psi_{i\mathbf{k}}|\Sigma(\omega)|\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}\rangle$
   are formed by contracting the spatial kernel with wavefunction values, and
   quasiparticle energies solve
   $\varepsilon^{QP} = \varepsilon^{DFT} + \langle \Sigma(\varepsilon^{QP}) - V_{xc} \rangle$
   by one of the solvers of §4.5.

Two chapters of machinery hang off this picture: *where the spatial axes live*
(Chapter 5: ISDF) and *how the time/frequency dependence is sampled* (Chapter 7).
The next three sections establish the scaling rules that force both designs.
