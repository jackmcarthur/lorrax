# 5.3 The fit, stated

Determining the interpolation vectors is a linear least-squares problem: minimize,
independently for each momentum transfer $\mathbf{q}$, the squared interpolation
error summed over all band pairs and k-points in the fitting window. Setting the
functional derivative to zero yields normal equations — one small linear system per
$\mathbf{q}$, shared by all bands:

$$
\boxed{\; C_q\, \zeta_q = Z_q \;}
\qquad
\sum_{\mu} C_{q,\nu\mu}\, \zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r}) = Z_{q,\nu}(\mathbf{r}).
$$

The coefficient matrices *can be derived* straightforwardly (the derivation is
standard and not reproduced here; see the references in Appendix F) and collapse into
products of **quasi-density matrices** evaluated at the interpolation points — this is
the form the code computes and the one worth remembering:

$$
P_{\mathbf{k},ss'}(\mathbf{r}_\nu, \mathbf{x}) \;=\; \sum_{n \in \text{window}}
\psi^*_{n\mathbf{k},s}(\mathbf{r}_\nu)\; \psi_{n\mathbf{k},s'}(\mathbf{x}),
$$

$$
C_{q,\nu\mu} = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{ss'} P^*_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s's}(\mathbf{r}_\nu,\mathbf{r}_\mu)\,
P_{\mathbf{k},ss'}(\mathbf{r}_\nu,\mathbf{r}_\mu),
\qquad
Z_{q,\nu}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{ss'} P^*_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s's}(\mathbf{r}_\nu,\mathbf{r})\,
P_{\mathbf{k},ss'}(\mathbf{r}_\nu,\mathbf{r}).
$$

Three structural facts do all the work:

1. **Band sums happen first** (T1). The $P_\mathbf{k}$ are single band sums over the
   window — never pair sums. All $O(n_b^2)$ pair information enters through products
   of two $P$'s, i.e. through $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ and $n_\mu \times N_r$
   matrices, and the wavefunctions themselves are needed only at the centroids
   ($C_q$) and on the grid once per fitting sweep ($Z_q$).
2. **The k-sums are lattice convolutions** (§4.3). Both expressions have the
   $\sum_\mathbf{k} A^*_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}} B_\mathbf{k}$ shape, so the code
   evaluates them for *all* $\mathbf{q}$ at once by FFT over the Born–von Kármán
   lattice — the fit inherits the $O(N_k \log N_k)$ scaling.
3. **One factorization, many right-hand sides.** $C_q$ ($n_\mu \times n_\mu$,
   Hermitian) is factorized once per $\mathbf{q}$ — Cholesky in the charge channel,
   where $C_q$ is positive semidefinite up to numerical rank deficiency (a small
   trace-proportional ridge regularizes it); the bispinor transverse channels are
   *indefinite* and take LU instead (§8.2). The factors are then back-solved against
   every column of $Z_q$. On a device mesh both the factorization and the solves run
   distributed (2D-blocked Cholesky, or the `distributed_cholesky` /
   `distributed_lu` backends of §12.3 at scale).

Finally, the Coulomb matrix in the ISDF basis — the object every later chapter
consumes — is assembled in reciprocal space from the fitted vectors,

$$
V_{q,\mu\nu} \;=\; \sum_\mathbf{G} \tilde z^*_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{G})\;
\tilde z_{q,\nu}(\mathbf{G}),
\qquad
\tilde z_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{G}) = \sqrt{v_q(\mathbf{G})}\;
\mathrm{FFT}\big[e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})\big],
$$

with the Coulomb kernel $v_q(\mathbf{G})$, its geometry-dependent truncation, and its
$\mathbf{q}\to 0$ subtleties deferred to Chapter 6. From here on, *"the Coulomb
interaction" means the dense matrix $V_{q,\mu\nu}$*, and the entire GW loop of
Chapter 4 runs on $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ objects.

> **Reader note.** Production runs never materialize the full intermediate tensors
> implied above: the fit streams over band chunks, grid chunks, and q-batches under a
> memory planner, and $\zeta$ lives on disk between stages. None of that changes a
> single equation; it is Chapter 12's business.
