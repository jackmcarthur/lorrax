# 5.1 The idea: interpolating pair densities

Every expensive object in Chapter 4 is built from **products of two wavefunctions at
the same point** — pair densities. For momentum transfer $\mathbf{q}$:

$$
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;=\; \psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r})\,
\psi_{n,\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}).
$$

There are $O(N^2)$ such products per k-point and each lives on the full grid of $N_r$
points: the pair-density *tensor* is the $O(N^3)$-sized wall every GW formulation hits.
But the products are massively redundant. All of them are built from the same
$O(N)$ orbitals, and orbitals in a given material have limited ways of overlapping:
the pair densities span a space of dimension far smaller than their count.

**Interpolative Separable Density Fitting (ISDF)** exploits this with a
deceptively simple ansatz — the entire method rests on this single equation:

$$
\boxed{\;
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;\approx\; \sum_{\mu=1}^{n_\mu}
\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})\; \rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}_\mu)
\;}
$$

A pair density anywhere is a linear combination of its **own values at a fixed set of
interpolation points** $\{\mathbf{r}_\mu\}$, with interpolation vectors
$\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$ that are *shared by all band pairs and k-points* — they
depend only on the momentum transfer $\mathbf{q}$. The band/k information enters only
through the cheap factor $\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}_\mu) =
\psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r}_\mu)\,\psi_{n,\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}_\mu)$
— wavefunction values *at the points*. This is what "separable" means in the name:
the $(mn\mathbf{k})$ dependence separates from the $\mathbf{r}$ dependence.

Two consequences make the rest of the code possible:

1. **Compression.** Storage falls from $O(n_k n_b^2 N_r)$ pair densities to
   $O(n_q\, n_\mu N_r)$ interpolation vectors plus wavefunction samples at
   $n_\mu$ points. Downstream, every two-point kernel of Chapter 4 becomes a dense
   $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ matrix.
2. **The two-sums rule survives.** Because the ansatz never mixes band indices —
   $\rho_{mn}$ on the left is interpolated from $\rho_{mn}$ on the right — band sums
   over $m$ and $n$ can still be performed independently at the interpolation points
   (T1, §4.2). A fitting scheme that entangled band pairs would have destroyed the
   $O(N^3)$ structure; this one is transparent to it.

**Where the points come from.** The $\{\mathbf{r}_\mu\}$ are chosen once, before the
GW run, by weighted k-means (CVT) clustering of the real-space grid with the charge
density of the relevant band window as weight (`lorrax-centroids`). Points concentrate
where orbitals actually overlap — bonds, lone pairs — and the *centroid* language in
the code and in this manual refers to these cluster centers. The count $n_\mu$ is the
basis-size dial of the whole method (§5.5); the symmetry post-processing that slightly
enlarges a requested set is §5.4.

What remains is to determine the $\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$. That is a linear
least-squares problem whose solution is stated (not derived) in §5.3 — after a short
detour that explains why LORRAX's spinor-first design makes the fit *better*, not
merely bigger.
