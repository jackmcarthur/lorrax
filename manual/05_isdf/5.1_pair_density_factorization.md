# 5.1 Exploiting the low-rank structure of pair densities

The familiar quantities in GW+BSE calculations (the dielectric matrix, the GW self energy) are all constructed from expensive sums of "pair densities":

$$
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;=\; \psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r})\,
\psi_{n,\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}).
$$

The full pair density tensor can be on the order of terabytes even for small calculations; a formalism that exploits its low rank structure can significantly accelerate GW+BSE calculations. 
Consider that, if $N_{k}=N_{q}$, there are $O(N_{b}^2 N_{k}^2)$ pair densities on $N_r$ grid points, but heuristically, their spatial structure should span generally the same space that can be described by $O(N_b)$ Wannier functions. We can achieve a rank-$O(N_b)$ representation of $\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r})$ with an ansatz:

$$
\boxed{\;
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;\approx\; \sum_{\mu=1}^{n_\mu}
\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})\; \rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}_\mu)
\;}
$$

a pair density anywhere is a linear combination of its own values at fixed
interpolation points $\{\mathbf{r}_\mu\}$, with vectors $\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$
shared by all band pairs and k-points and depending only on $\mathbf{q}$. Accuracy is controlled by the choice of $N_{\mu}$; in typical calculations, sub-meV accuracy is obtained with $N_{\mu}\sim 10\text{-}14 N_{b}$, thus giving a low-rank approximation to $\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r})$ with rank $O(N_b)$.
(Note the momentum transfer convention of $\psi^*_{\mathbf{k-q}}$ ensures that $\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$ has the form of
a phase $e^{+i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$ multiplied by a cell-periodic part $z_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$.)

Storage falls from $O(n_k n_b^2 N_r)$ pair densities to $O(n_q n_\mu N_r)$ vectors
plus wavefunction values at the points, and all two-point objects, e.g. $G_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r,r'},\omega)$ and $W_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{r,r'},\omega)$, can be represented on the
interpolation points alone, where each becomes a dense $n_\mu \times n_\mu$ matrix.

The points are chosen once, before the GW run, by k-means (CVT) clustering of the
grid weighted by the charge density of the band window (`lorrax-centroids`); they
concentrate where orbitals overlap, and "centroid" refers to these cluster centers.
$n_\mu$ is the basis-size dial (§5.5); symmetry post-processing enlarges a
requested set slightly (§5.4). Determining $\zeta$ is a linear least-squares
problem, stated in §5.3.
