# 5.5 Choosing the rank

The centroid count $n_\mu$ is the basis-size dial of the entire calculation — the
analogue of a plane-wave cutoff, and the first thing to converge in any new system.

**Rules of thumb.** Convergence sets in around $n_\mu \approx 8\text{–}12$ points per
band in the fitting window; equivalently, a few hundred points per handful of atoms
for typical semiconductors. The cost of generosity is quadratic-to-cubic (all
$(\mu,\nu)$ matrices grow as $n_\mu^2$, factorizations as $n_\mu^3$), so the practical
strategy is a short convergence sweep on the quantity of interest (band gap, a QP
level) rather than a defensive large value. Remember the orbit-closure surplus of
§5.4 when comparing counts.

**What ISDF error looks like.** Under-resolved ISDF produces *smooth, systematic*
shifts of quasiparticle energies that decay steadily as $n_\mu$ grows — one clean
knob, one clean convergence curve:

If disagreement with a reference calculation instead *plateaus* while $n_\mu$
increases, the basis is not the culprit — look at conventions and cutoffs
(Appendix E ranks the usual suspects, starting with `bare_coulomb_cutoff`).

**Interaction with the band window.** The fit targets pair densities of the window
actually used (`nval`, `ncond`, `nband`); enlarging the band count without revisiting
$n_\mu$ dilutes the basis. Sweep them together, in the same direction.

**Forward pointer.** In bispinor calculations there are *four* densities to fit, each
with its own rank; the charge:transverse ratio (recommended ≈3:1) and its cost model
are §8.2.
