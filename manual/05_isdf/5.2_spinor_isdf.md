# 5.2 Spinors make the fit better

With spin-orbit coupling every band is a two-component spinor,
$\psi_{n\mathbf{k}} = (\psi_{n\mathbf{k},\uparrow}, \psi_{n\mathbf{k},\downarrow})$,
and the object that couples to the Coulomb interaction is the **spin-traced** pair
density — the physical charge:

$$
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;=\; \sum_{s\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}}
\psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},s}(\mathbf{r})\, \psi_{n,\mathbf{k},s}(\mathbf{r})
\;=\; \sum_{\alpha\beta}\,\psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}(\mathbf{r})\,
\sigma^0_{\alpha\beta}\, \psi_{n,\mathbf{k},\beta}(\mathbf{r}),
$$

with $\sigma^0$ the identity in spin space (the notation anticipates Chapter 8, where
the Pauli matrices $\sigma^{1,2,3}$ generate three more densities).

The practical point is that LORRAX fits **one scalar field per band pair** — the
trace — rather than the two (or four) spin components separately. Because
$\uparrow\!\uparrow$ and $\downarrow\!\downarrow$ products are summed *before* the
ISDF ansatz is imposed, one degree of freedom per pair is removed from the target and
its noise cannot leak into the fit: at fixed $n_\mu$, the spin-traced fit is strictly
easier than fitting components, and in practice measurably more accurate. The
interpolation vectors $\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$ are **spin-independent** — one set
serves all spin channels.

One subtlety, stated here so §5.3's formulas don't surprise: although only the traced
density is fitted, the *least-squares error metric* couples all four spin channels of
the underlying quasi-density matrices, including the off-diagonal
$P_{\uparrow\downarrow}$, $P_{\downarrow\uparrow}$ — the fitting matrices in §5.3
carry an internal spin trace of the form
$\sum_{ss'} P^*_{s's} P_{ss'}$, a Frobenius product over the $2\times 2$ spin
structure. This falls out of the algebra automatically; users never handle spin
indices.

This is also the honest place to restate the cost of spinor-first design (§1.2,
§1.3): a nominally spinless material like silicon still runs with two-component
wavefunctions — its spin-off-diagonal blocks are simply (numerically) zero and no
computation is skipped on their account. What LORRAX buys with that constant factor is
a single code path in which SOC is never an afterthought, and a clean on-ramp to the
four-density bispinor formalism of Chapter 8.
