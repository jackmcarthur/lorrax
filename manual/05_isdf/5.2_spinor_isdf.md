# 5.2 Spinors improve the fit

With spin-orbit coupling every band is a two-component spinor, and the object
coupling to the Coulomb interaction is the spin-traced pair density

$$
\rho_{mn\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{q};\mathbf{r}) \;=\;
\sum_{\alpha\beta}\,\psi^*_{m,\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\alpha}(\mathbf{r})\,
\sigma^0_{\alpha\beta}\, \psi_{n,\mathbf{k},\beta}(\mathbf{r}),
$$

$\sigma^0$ the spin identity (the Pauli matrices generate three more densities in
Ch. 8). LORRAX fits this one scalar field per pair rather than the spin components
separately: summing $\uparrow\uparrow + \downarrow\downarrow$ before imposing the
ansatz removes a degree of freedom from the target, so at fixed $n_\mu$ the traced
fit is strictly easier and in practice more accurate. The vectors
$\zeta_{q,\mu}(\mathbf{r})$ are spin-independent.

Although only the trace is fitted, the least-squares metric couples all four spin
channels of the quasi-density matrices, including $P_{\uparrow\downarrow}$: the
fitting matrices of §5.3 carry the Frobenius spin trace
$\sum_{ss'} P^*_{s's} P_{ss'}$.

A nominally spinless material still runs with two-component wavefunctions and pays
that constant factor (§1.3); in exchange SOC is native throughout and the
four-density bispinor formalism of Chapter 8 is a direct extension.
