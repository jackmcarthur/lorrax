# 5.4 Symmetry and orbit closure

For the symmetry machinery to act on the ISDF basis, the point set
$\{\mathbf{r}_\mu\}$ must be a union of orbits closed under the space group (plus
time reversal where applicable). The centroid generator closes each k-means orbit,
snapping images to the FFT grid, so a request for 600 points returns somewhat more;
the surplus points carry real fitting information.

With a closed set, fitting matrices at symmetry-equivalent momentum transfers are
related exactly, so $C_q$ is factorized and $\zeta_q$ solved only for $\mathbf{q}$
in the irreducible zone, with $V_q$ and $\Sigma_x$ unfolded from it: a ~6–12×
reduction of the most expensive stages for a typical hexagonal cell. The cascade
activates automatically when the set is orbit-closed; the log states whether it
engaged and, if not, which orbit failed.

The quasi-density accumulation (§5.3's $P_\mathbf{k}$ sums) runs over the full
k-lattice; reduction there is possible in principle but not implemented. Unfolding
equations and the reduced-vs-not ledger: Appendix A.
